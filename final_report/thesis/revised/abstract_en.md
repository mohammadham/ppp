# Abstract

With the expansion of e-health systems, telemedicine, and the Internet of Medical Things, the secure transmission of medical images over open networks has become a fundamental challenge in health information security. Owing to their high volume, extensive redundancy, and strong correlation between adjacent pixels, medical images call for protection mechanisms that go beyond classical text-oriented encryption; patient metadata is also in need of independent protection. This thesis designs an integrated framework for enhancing the security and privacy of medical images that combines three components in a single processing chain: deep U-Net segmentation for identifying sensitive regions and extracting content-dependent statistical features, a five-dimensional hyper-chaotic system for generating pseudo-random sequences used in the confusion and diffusion phases, and saliency-based steganography for imperceptibly embedding compressed patient metadata into visually unattended regions. The present version focuses on design and the elaboration of the evaluation plan: experiments are defined on public datasets of retinal images (DRIVE and RITE), brain MRI (BraTS), and chest X-rays, with Shannon entropy, adjacent-pixel correlation, NPCR, UACI, PSNR, SSIM, and execution time as evaluation criteria, and with comparative scenarios against selected methods from the literature. Based on theoretical analyses and foundational studies, the encryption core is expected to reach an entropy close to the ideal value of 8 bits, differential measures within standard bounds (NPCR around 99.6 percent and UACI around 33.3 percent), and the steganography module a PSNR above 40 dB; final confirmation of these values depends on the practical implementation and analysis of the planned experiments. By integrating deep learning, chaos theory, and adaptive steganography, the proposed framework takes a step toward privacy-aware, secure e-health and Internet of Medical Things systems.

**Keywords:** Medical Image Security, Hyper-chaotic Encryption, Deep Learning, U-Net, Steganography, Privacy, Internet of Medical Things
